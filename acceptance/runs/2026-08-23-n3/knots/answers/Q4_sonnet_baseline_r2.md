`calculate_aird` and `calculate_aird_raw` (`src/complexity.rs:1433` and `:1463`) implement the exact same weighted-sum formula — same five weighted terms (cognitive ×55, sloc ×15, nesting ×15, test ×15, minus doc ×15, plus state-coupling ×10) — but they differ in *where the `.min(1.0)` normalization caps are applied*, and in the output type:

- **`calculate_aird`**: caps `cognitive_norm` and `sloc_norm` at 1.0 before weighting, then clamps the final result to `[0, 100]` and rounds to a `u32`. This is the score that's actually stored on `FunctionMetrics` (`lib.rs:194`), gates thresholds (`--aird-threshold`), and gets printed everywhere.
- **`calculate_aird_raw`**: leaves `cognitive_norm` and `sloc_norm` **uncapped** (only nesting/test/doc/coupling stay clamped), returns an unclamped `f64`, and can exceed 100 for very large/complex functions.

Why anyone wants the second one: once a function's cognitive complexity or SLOC is far past the normalization denominator (75 and 200 respectively), the capped score pins at 100 and *stays* at 100 no matter how much worse the function gets — or, more importantly for someone refactoring, it stays at 100 even as they make real progress shrinking it, as long as it's still above the cap. That's a dead gauge for anyone mid-refactor on an egregious function: the CI-gate number tells them nothing until they cross back under the cap. `calculate_aird_raw` gives a monotonically decreasing number in that regime, so tools like `format_aird_breakdown`/`aird_tips` (`src/output.rs:69`, `:141`) can show "raw AIRD (uncapped): N" and let a user see incremental improvement even while the official capped score is still stuck at 100. It's explicitly a progress-tracking signal, not the enforcement metric — the comment at `complexity.rs:1458-1462` says exactly this, and the test `test_aird_raw_tracks_progress_above_cap` (`complexity.rs:2050`) pins the behavior: capped AIRD identical before/after, raw AIRD decreases.
