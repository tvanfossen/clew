Both live in `src/complexity.rs` and share the identical weighted formula over the same six inputs (cognitive, sloc, nesting, test_score, doc_score, state_coupling), but they differ in exactly one place:

- **`calculate_aird`** (line 1433) normalizes `cognitive` and `sloc` with `.min(1.0)` caps before weighting, then rounds and clamps the final result to `[0, 100]`, returning a `u32`. This is the score used for the CI gate / reported metric.
- **`calculate_aird_raw`** (line 1463) uses the *same* formula but leaves `cognitive_norm` and `sloc_norm` uncapped (only `nesting`, `test`, `doc`, `coupling` stay clamped, per the inline comment "nesting rarely exceeds cap; keep clamped"). It returns an unclamped `f64` that can exceed 100 when a function's cognitive complexity or SLOC blows past the normalization denominators (75 and 200 respectively).

Why anyone wants the second one: the doxygen comment on `calculate_aird_raw` states its purpose directly — it's for **progress tracking on very large/complex functions**. Once a function's cognitive or SLOC value is large enough to saturate the capped AIRD at 100, `calculate_aird` will report 100 both before and after a partial refactor, giving no feedback that the refactor helped. `calculate_aird_raw` keeps climbing above 100 uncapped, so as the function is incrementally simplified the raw score visibly decreases even while the capped, gate-facing score is still pinned at the ceiling — it exists purely to give a non-flat signal in that saturated regime.

Its only callers are `aird_tips` and `raw_aird_suffix` in `src/output.rs`, both output/reporting helpers — `raw_aird_suffix`'s brief confirms this: it emits the `raw AIRD (uncapped): N` line "once cognitive or sloc has hit its cap," i.e. it's purely a display/diagnostic aid layered on top of the same underlying formula, not a second scoring path used for gating.
