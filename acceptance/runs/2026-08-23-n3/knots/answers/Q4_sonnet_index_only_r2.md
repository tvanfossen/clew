The two functions live side by side in `src/complexity.rs` (`calculate_aird` at line 1433, `calculate_aird_raw` at 1463) and share the exact same weighted formula — cognitive×55 + sloc×15 + nesting×15 + test×15 − doc×15 + coupling×10 — over the same six normalized inputs. The difference is entirely in the normalization:

- **`calculate_aird`** clamps every one of `cognitive_norm`, `sloc_norm`, etc. to `.min(1.0)` before weighting, then rounds and clamps the final sum to `[0, 100]`, returning a `u32`. This is the score used everywhere else (`collect_function_metrics` calls it) — it's the stable, bounded 0–100 metric meant for CI gating and reporting.
- **`calculate_aird_raw`** leaves `cognitive_norm` and `sloc_norm` *uncapped* (only `nesting_norm`, `test_norm`, `doc_norm`, `coupling_norm` stay clamped — the comment even flags this: "nesting rarely exceeds cap; keep clamped"), and returns an unclamped `f64` that can exceed 100.

**Why anyone wants the second one:** it exists to solve a specific blind spot in the first. Once a function's cognitive complexity crosses 75 or SLOC crosses 200, `calculate_aird` pins at 100 — the ceiling — and *stays* at 100 no matter how much worse the function gets. If you're refactoring a huge, gnarly function, the capped score gives you zero feedback: it reads "100" before and after every intermediate change until you've dropped below the cap. `calculate_aird_raw` keeps growing past 100, so it can still distinguish "still bad" from "getting better" while the capped score is saturated. Concretely, it's consumed by `raw_aird_suffix` (in `src/output.rs`), which only prints the "raw AIRD (uncapped): N" line when `cognitive >= 75 || sloc >= 200` — i.e., exactly when the capped metric has lost its resolution — giving a progress signal during refactoring that the gated/reported score can no longer provide.
