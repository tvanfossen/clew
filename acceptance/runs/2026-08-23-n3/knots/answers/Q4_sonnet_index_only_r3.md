Both `calculate_aird` and `calculate_aird_raw` in `src/complexity.rs` (lines 1433 and 1463) run the identical weighted formula over the same six inputs (cognitive, sloc, nesting, test_score, doc_score, state_coupling) — same weights (55/15/15/15/-15/10) and same normalization denominators (75, 200, 8, 20, 10, 12). The difference is entirely in the clamping:

- **`calculate_aird`** applies `.min(1.0)` to every one of the per-input normalized ratios *before* summing, then rounds and clamps the final sum to `[0, 100]`, returning a `u32`. Any input that blows past its denominator (e.g. cognitive complexity of 300 against the 75 cap) gets capped to a contribution of exactly 1.0 — it can never push the score past what a merely-at-cap function would produce.
- **`calculate_aird_raw`** skips the `.min(1.0)` caps on cognitive and sloc specifically (nesting/test/doc/coupling stay clamped, per the inline comment "nesting rarely exceeds cap"), only floors the total at 0, and returns an uncapped `f64` that can exceed 100.

Why anyone wants the second one: `calculate_aird`'s own doc comment says AIRD is the CI-gate-facing 0–100 score, but that ceiling creates a blind spot — a function with cognitive=80 and one with cognitive=800 both get pinned at the same capped score of 100, so refactoring a monster function shows *zero visible progress* on the gated metric until it drops below the cap. `calculate_aird_raw` exists specifically for that case: its callers are `aird_tips` and `raw_aird_suffix` in `src/output.rs`, and `raw_aird_suffix`'s own brief confirms the intent — it prints the line `"raw AIRD (uncapped): N"` only once cognitive or sloc has actually hit its cap, giving the user a number that keeps decreasing as they simplify a maxed-out function, even though the official capped AIRD score stays frozen at 100.

There's a third related function worth flagging so it isn't confused for a third "AIRD calculation": `apply_aird_ce_multiplier`, which isn't an alternate formula at all — it's a post-pass that rescales an already-computed (capped) AIRD by a file-level efferent-coupling multiplier, applied later once the whole-corpus import graph is available, since `calculate_aird` runs eagerly per-function before that graph exists.
